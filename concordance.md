No complete copy of *The Concordance* has ever been verified.

References appear throughout newsletters, correspondence, lecture transcripts, and observation reports dating back to at least 1968.

Researchers remain divided regarding its nature.

Some believe *The Concordance* was a single text.

Others argue it was an evolving collection compiled by multiple authors over several decades.

No consensus exists.


## Surviving Fragments

The Archives currently maintain 37 materials believed to be associated with *The Concordance*.

These include:

* Diagrammatic pages
* Handwritten notes
* Partial transcripts
* Marginal annotations
* Symbolic indexes
* Observation records

Most survive only in fragmentary form.

Several contradict one another directly.


## Fragment 11-B

Recovered from a collection of lecture notes donated in 1998.

![Fragment 11-B](/assets/images/archives/chart.jpg)

The fragment consists of a single page containing a geometric diagram and the following message on the back:

*"A thing observed repeatedly begins to observe in return."*

The surrounding pages have not been recovered.


## Fragment 22-F

Referenced in newsletters between 1986 and 1994.

No verified copy survives.

Descriptions differ substantially between sources.

Several accounts mention a diagram resembling a map.

Others describe a list of names.


## Recurring Phrases

The following statements appear throughout multiple fragments:

* Observation is participation.
* The signal precedes the receiver.
* The map becomes a prison when mistaken for the territory.

Researchers disagree whether these statements originate from a common source.


## The Concordance Hypothesis

First proposed publicly by Orion Hypersoul during the late 1970s.

The hypothesis suggests that recurring structures exist beneath seemingly unrelated events, symbols, experiences, and historical developments.

According to the hypothesis, *The Concordance* did not create these patterns.

It merely attempted to describe them.

## Ongoing Research

Current efforts focus on:

* Recovering additional fragments
* Comparing symbolic systems
* Identifying anonymous contributors
* Establishing provenance
* Determining whether a complete version ever existed

No definitive conclusions have been reached.

The Archives Department continues to accept submissions.
